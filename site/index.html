Separating disease priors from patient evidence

Two models can reach the same accuracy and still rank diagnoses differently. DiagRare-Bench fits a Plackett–Luce model to each model's ranked differentials and reports how strongly the ranking follows case-specific findings once disease prevalence is held fixed.

Leaderboard

Checkpoints are ranked by γevidence, the evidence coefficient from the 984-case benchmark. A checkpoint is ranked only when its coefficient is identifiable, at least half of its outputs are usable, and top-1 accuracy is at least 5%. Nothing is imputed.

How to read it

γevidence is the coefficient on case-specific evidence in a two-feature Plackett–Luce model fitted to a model's ranked differential, with disease prevalence as the second feature. Larger values mean that stronger findings move a diagnosis up the ranking more, at fixed prevalence.

γprior is the matching coefficient on prevalence. It is descriptive and can be negative.

The coefficient depends on the evidence scale, prompt, candidate set and decoding rule. It is reported next to accuracy, and it is not a clinical safety score.
