Separating disease priors from patient evidence in language models for clinical diagnosis

DiagRare-Bench asks a targeted question: when patient evidence changes, how much does a model's diagnostic ranking move with that evidence after the specified disease-prior feature is accounted for?

Method

Evidence responsiveness from ranked diagnoses

Given a ranked differential, we fit a Plackett-Luce choice model with a disease-prior feature and a case-specific evidence feature. The estimator uses ranked outputs only and describes the observed decision rule rather than the model's internal representation.

The coefficient is comparative rather than absolute: its magnitude depends on the evidence scale, prompt, candidate set, and decoding rule. Across datasets with different evidence representations, the paper compares model ordering rather than raw coefficient magnitude.

U_m(d,x) = γ_prior p(d) + γ_evidence e(d,x) ∂ log odds(a ≻ b) / ∂Δe = γ_evidence
01 · PrimaryEstimate γ_evidence on 984 structured diagnostic cases.
02 · TransferRepeat the rank analysis on CUPCase and RareArena with a different evidence proxy.
03 · RandomizeAssign evidence strength and stated prevalence independently in a 3 x 3 experiment.
04 · RevisePresent evidence sequentially and measure recovery after an initial competing diagnosis.
Main findings

The rank-derived coefficient predicts behavior under independent evidence changes.

The same-task accuracy association is descriptive. The stronger tests change the case distribution or the evidence itself.

DiagRare-Bench is an evaluation benchmark, not a clinical validation study. A high evidence-responsiveness score should not be interpreted as evidence that a model is safe or reliable for clinical deployment.
